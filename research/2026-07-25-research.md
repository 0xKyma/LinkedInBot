### ✅ Item 1 — arXiv:2607.10347 "A Framework for Managing the Models of Engineered Quantum Systems" (QSysMM/QSysML on SysML v2)
**Score: 20/25**

The paper, submitted July 11, 2026, proposes QSysMM — a framework with four concerns (ontological, abstraction, composition, and exposure) — and within it defines a Quantum Systems Modelling Language (QSysML) on the SysML v2 technology stack, closing with a proposal to mature this into full model management for quantum systems.
 
The core research problem identified is that model transformations in quantum systems can silently remove crucial quantum properties like superposition and entanglement without triggering any error detection — a vulnerability Siyuan Ji of Loughborough University and colleagues say current synchronization methods are insufficient to address.
 
Their proposed QSysML is built on the existing SysML v2 technology stack, aiming for pragmatic integration with established classical engineering workflows.


**Why it beats the others:** It is the freshest item (July 11–14, 2026), is genuinely novel for LinkedIn (no prior SE community discussion found), and opens a high-debate question — *Is SysML v2's extension model the right vehicle for quantum systems engineering, or are we just stapling new problems onto a classical language?* — that will resonate with Photi's aerospace and defense audience encountering quantum sensor and computing programs.
