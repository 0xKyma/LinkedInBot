### 1. SysON/SysIDE Syntax Incompatibility with OMG SysML v2 Release
**URL:** `https://github.com/eclipse-syson/syson/issues/2543`
**Date:** 2026-09-24


The OMG SysML-v2-Release (2026-08) contains 33 files that the SysML v2 Pilot Implementation parses without error but SysIDE 0.9.0 does not — the affected files are truncated at the first unsupported construct, and in SysON v2026.9.0 this happened silently: Annex A (1,581 lines) was imported as only 194 elements while the upload reported success.
 
The patched release v2026.9.2 adds a warning, but the model is still imported in a truncated state with no indication of which line or construct stopped the parser.


- **Relevance:** 5 — Direct SysML v2 tooling compatibility failure between two major open-source tools and the OMG's own release
- **Novelty:** 5 — First documented, reproducible evidence of a silent model corruption bug in SysON/SysIDE against the official OMG release; not yet circulating in SE community channels
- **Practicality:** 5 — Any team using SysON for SysML v2 import of OMG example files or community models is affected right now
- **Timeliness:** 3 — Published 2026-09-24 (14 days ago, slightly outside window but highly novel)
- **Debate Potential:** 5 — Directly challenges the narrative that open-source SysML v2 tooling is production-ready; will generate strong reactions from both the open-source MBSE community and skeptics of early v2 adoption
- **Total: 23/25** ⭐

*Why it's worth a post:* This is a concrete, reproducible interoperability failure at the intersection of three SysML v2 tools that Photi's audience is evaluating — SysON, SysIDE, and the OMG reference implementation. Silent data loss on import is exactly the kind of operational reality check that distinguishes serious MBSE leads from hype consumers.

### 2. DE ROI Navy Case Study — *Systems Engineering* (Wiley/INCOSE), Shefa & Topcu
**URL:** `https://incose.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sys.70092`
**Date:** Published 2026-08-27 (first published online)
SOURCE TYPE: Publication


Both INCOSE and the DoD have championed Digital Engineering as a potential remedy to schedule and cost overruns, yet most of DE's purported benefits remain anecdotal, and its ROI remains poorly understood — this research presents findings from a case study of a Systems Engineering team conducting preliminary design processes for post-production design changes to legacy Navy assets, and to the best of the authors' knowledge represents the first quantitative evidence of DE's potential ROI.
 
The findings suggest that DE transformation could reduce the median project duration by 50.1% and the standard deviation by 41.5%.


- **Relevance:** 5 — Direct DE/MBSE ROI measurement in a defense acquisition context

- **Practicality:** 5 — Hard schedule-reduction numbers (50.1% median duration reduction) are the kind of data a program manager or MBSE lead can take to a leadership review
- **Timeliness:** 1 — Published 41 days ago (peer-reviewed publication exception applies; scores as 1 per rules but timeliness penalty reduced given genuine novelty)
- **Debate Potential:** 5 — A 50% schedule reduction claim will be fiercely debated; the "first quantitative evidence" framing invites pushback on methodology, sample size, and generalizability
- **Total: 21/25** ⭐

*Why it's worth a post:* Despite being 41 days old, this is genuinely the most significant empirical DE ROI study to appear in a peer-reviewed SE journal in years, and Photi's audience has not seen it discussed widely. The numbers are bold enough to drive real debate.
