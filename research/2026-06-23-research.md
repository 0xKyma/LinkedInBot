### Candidate 2 — USAF CCA: Hardware and Autonomy Software Deliberately Decoupled into Separate Acquisitions
**Source:** Breaking Defense / Air Force official / airforce-technology.com
**URLs:**
- https://breakingdefense.com/2026/06/air-force-cca-drone-wingman-anduril-general-atomics-selection/
- https://www.airforce-technology.com/news/usaf-cca-contracts-software/

**Date:** 17–22 June 2026 (1–6 days ago)

**Summary:** 
The Air Force has selected General Atomics and Anduril to build the US military's first Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA); in addition, Anduril, Shield AI and RTX subsidiary Collins Aerospace have been tapped to continue a competition that will determine who provides the autonomy software for the loyal wingman drones.
 
Under these new arrangements, the Air Force is separating the procurement of physical aircraft hardware from the autonomy software required for its missions.


**SE/Systems Thinking Angle:** This is one of the most architecturally significant procurement decisions in decades: 
these distinct efforts validate acquisition transformation principles to secure a critical operational advantage — decoupling hardware from software. By treating mission autonomy as "software sold separately," the Air Force ensures warfighters receive state-of-the-art physical platforms alongside agile, easily updatable software, effectively breaking traditional procurement molds.
 The non-obvious SE angle: 
all participants must comply with the government-owned Autonomy Government Reference Architecture (A-GRA), which underpins the open systems approach and supports integration of software across multiple hardware platforms.
 This is a live, real-world experiment in whether a **government-mandated interface standard** (A-GRA) can hold a system-of-systems together when hardware and software suppliers are independently competed — exactly the interface management problem MBSE practitioners model but rarely see stress-tested at this scale.

Despite record global energy investment reaching $3.3 trillion, energy transition progress is fragmenting as geopolitical tensions, infrastructure bottlenecks and rising energy demand expose vulnerabilities in the global transition; the WEF ETI 2026 tracks progress across 120 countries, with weakening policy certainty and financing conditions emerging as the sharpest constraints on momentum.

**SE/Systems Thinking Angle:** The non-obvious angle is a **system optimisation mismatch**: the global energy system has been optimised for generation (capacity added, costs driven down), but the integration layer — transmission and grid connection — was treated as a background concern.

 This is a global-scale **interface bottleneck** — precisely the kind of integration failure SE practitioners are taught to model at SoS level but which appears to have been absent in how nations planned the energy transition.
