### Candidate 1 — arXiv:2609.16252
**"Models as Governed Interfaces for AI-Native MBSE: Read-Side Adequacy and Write-Side Admissibility"**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16252 | 30 Jul 2026 | Accepted at MODELS 2026


The paper opens from the premise that while SysML v2 models are now programmatically accessible, an AI reader querying a structurally complete model still runs into absent derivation chains, untagged epistemic status, and missing provenance — and "faced with these gaps, it does not abstain; it fills them from training data, a source that is neither verifiable nor governed."



The paper names this missing property "epistemic adequacy" and proposes it as a data-architecture pattern in two halves: "Read-side adequacy lets derivation, status, and provenance answer a query rather than invite a guess; write-side admissibility gates an AI contribution before it enters the record."



The architecture space runs from inline metadata extension up to a substrate-native multi-model store, over which the authors propose the Governed-Query Architecture Framework, governing agent participation through viewpoint conventions engineers already use.



The paper is accepted at the 29th International Conference on Model Driven Engineering Languages and Systems (MODELS), 4–9 October 2026, Malaga, Spain, as a New Ideas and Emerging Results (NIER) paper.


- **Relevance: 5** — SysML v2 + AI governance directly at the intersection of the audience's live concerns.
- **Novelty: 5** — Introduces "epistemic adequacy" as a new data-architecture concept; probes the Apollo 11 SysML v2 reconstruction as empirical evidence; not yet widely discussed in SE community channels.
- **Practicality: 4** — Practitioner-applicable framework (five criteria), though still conceptual with no implemented tooling.
- **Timeliness: 1** — Submitted 30 Jul 2026 (~57 days ago). Accepted conference paper → timeliness penalty reduced per publication rules. Timeliness = 1 (publication exception applies; genuinely novel content not yet widely circulated).
- **Debate Potential: 5** — Directly challenges the field's assumption that SysML v2 machine-readability = AI-readiness. Forces debate on model governance that almost no one is currently having.
- **Total: 20/25** ✅

### Candidate 2 — arXiv:2606.29006
**"Automated SysML-Based Verification of Discipline-Specific Models"**
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29006 | 27 Jun 2026 | MBDA Systems × Loughborough University


The paper's diagnosis is pointed: current SysML-based verification approaches have two systematic flaws — "they are developed in a tool-specific manner using proprietary APIs, limiting portability," and "they focus on performance properties modelled via parametric diagrams, overlooking behavioural and interface properties that also require verification."



The proposed solution is a verification process tailored to model-based verification, informed by common SysML tool capabilities and the UML Testing Profile. "A mixed-method approach combining literature research and stakeholder interviews was used to derive validated stakeholder needs," and the process was demonstrated end-to-end in two independent SysML tool-chains to evidence tool-agnosticism, verifying behavioral and interface requirements including ordering, timing, and state-based responses.


- **Relevance: 5** — Directly addresses SysML verification practice with a concrete tool-agnostic methodology.
- **Novelty: 4** — The tool-agnosticism + behavioral/interface gap framing is fresh; the UML Testing Profile connection to MBSE is under-explored in the community.
- **Practicality: 5** — A practitioner at MBDA Systems co-authored this; the process is demonstrated in real toolchains. Directly adoptable.
- **Timeliness: 1** — Submitted 27 Jun 2026 (~91 days ago). Publication exception applies: not widely discussed in SE channels.
- **Debate Potential: 4** — The critique of parametric-only V&V will land hard with practitioners who've relied on SysML parametric diagrams for years.
- **Total: 19/25** ✅
