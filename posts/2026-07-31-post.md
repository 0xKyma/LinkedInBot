## Prompt-Strategy-Driven SysML v2 Artefact Generation Using LLMs

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle
A new peer-reviewed study just told us what most of us suspected: we don't actually know how reliable LLMs are at generating SysML v2 artefacts.

The paper (MDPI AI, published 23 July) tests prompt strategies for LLM-assisted SysML v2 generation on a traction battery case study. The headline finding is blunt. Reliability "remains insufficiently understood," especially around prompt-strategy selection, model-dependent variability, and task-specific performance.

That matters if you're piloting AI in your modelling workflow right now. The same prompt can produce a clean artefact on one model and a subtly broken one on another. And you won't always catch the difference by eye.

So before you wire an LLM into your MBSE toolchain, build a way to measure output quality per task and per model. Don't assume it generalises.

## Automated Semantic Fault Localization in SysML v2 (KG-Augmented LLMs)

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle
Your model checker catches syntax. It doesn't catch a valid model that says the wrong thing.

A new INCOSE IS 2026 paper targets exactly that gap. It builds a human-in-the-loop framework using knowledge-graph augmented LLMs to find semantic faults in SysML v2: models that pass syntactic validation but violate domain-specific engineering constraints. Fine-tuning pushed semantic fault repair from under 3% to over 91%.

That's the class of fault that survives every automated check and gets caught late, in review, or worse, in test.

If you run MBSE today, this points at a real hole in your toolchain. Syntactic validation is solved. Semantic validation is mostly still a human doing a careful read.

What semantic faults have slipped through your model reviews and only surfaced downstream?

An INCOSE IS 2026 paper uses SysML v2's textual syntax for compiler-like validation, then adds knowledge-graph augmented LLMs to catch faults that are syntactically valid but semantically wrong. Reported semantic fault repair jumped from under 3% to over 91% after fine-tuning.

Watch which vendors move on semantic validation next. That's where the investment signal is. Syntactic checking is commodity now. The differentiator is catching the faults that pass every automated check today.

Model quality has always come down to who is willing to do the careful read. The tools that automate that read will own the market.
