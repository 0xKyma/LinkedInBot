## Australian Federal Budget — Systems Thinking / Policy as Architecture

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle
Pulling one lever in a complex adaptive system and expecting a single, predictable output is a classic modelling failure.

The 2026/27 Australian Federal Budget limits negative gearing on established residential properties and removes the 50% CGT discount from 1 July 2027. The stated goal: more homes for first buyers, less investor dominance of the established property market.

The system responded with behaviours the policy model likely underweighted. Grandfathered properties give existing investors a stronger reason to hold, not sell, which tightens listings rather than expanding them. The CGT changes apply across all asset classes, so the boundary bleeds into adjacent subsystems the policy wasn't targeting. And the new supply this intervention depends on requires construction capacity, planning approvals, and materials costs it doesn't touch.

Revenue from these measures doesn't land until 2029. That feedback delay makes cause-and-effect attribution nearly impossible by the time the data arrives.

We see this in system architectures too: optimise one node, leave the interface constraints uncontrolled, and the emergent behaviour surprises everyone.
