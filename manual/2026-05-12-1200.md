## MBSE Fundamentals Training / Custom Profiles / DEWG Integration

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle

Custom profiles are one of the most consequential modelling decisions a programme makes, and most teams treat them as an afterthought.

When you define a custom profile, you encode how your organisation represents systems knowledge. That encoding shapes what queries return, what comparisons hold, and what integrates downstream. Get it wrong early and you're refactoring assumptions, not just models.

The pattern I see in training is that engineers understand tooling mechanics quickly. What takes longer is understanding that profile design is a systems engineering decision, not a configuration task. The thinking has to come before the modelling.

If your programme is still treating custom profiles as something to sort out after the model is populated, the integration debt is already accumulating.

### Option 2 — Industry/trend angle (The adoption gap)

The DEWG is doing serious work on defence-wide model integration. The gap between that ambition and programme-level practice is still wide.

Running MBSE Fundamentals training this week made that gap visible again. Engineers are making custom profile decisions programme by programme, with no shared semantic foundation beneath them. Every bespoke profile becomes a future integration problem.

A defence-wide shared profile, defined as part of the DEWG effort, would give programmes something to build from rather than something to reconcile later. Teams build models today against bespoke foundations, and the cost of connecting them grows with every programme that starts without common semantics. That is a standards problem, not a tooling problem.

The DEWG has the reach to make that happen. Defining a shared custom profile should be part of the mandate.

### Option 3 — Contrarian angle (The mainstream fix makes it worse)

Teaching MBSE fundamentals and pushing tool adoption at the same time is producing a specific failure mode: engineers who can build models but cannot explain the decisions inside them.

The standard fix is more training. More hours in the tool, more worked examples, more model templates. But templates built on undefined or inconsistent custom profiles just scale the problem faster.

Programmes end up with models that look coherent and cannot integrate with anything outside their own project boundary. The DEWG's integration ambition runs directly into this. You cannot federate models that teams built to no common semantic standard.

The discipline needs to settle the profile question at an enterprise level before it trains another cohort to model against bespoke foundations.
