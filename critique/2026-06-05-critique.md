## POST 1 — OMG SysML v2 Certification Launch (Announcement / Career Angle)

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle

OMG just launched the first official SysML v2 certification, and for most practitioners the timing question is real: does this credential matter enough to pursue now?

The entry-level "Model User" credential is live as of June 2. It covers reading and interpreting SysML v2 models. The next tier, "Model Builder Fundamental," lands in early 2027. Intermediate and Advanced follow after that.

People who wrote the spec developed the exam content. That matters. OCSMP had years of drift between what the exam tested and what practitioners actually did. Starting from the spec itself is a better foundation.

My honest take: if you are actively working in SysML v2 or planning to, the Model User cert is low-risk and high-signal right now. Early credentials in a new series carry more weight than the same credential three years from now when everyone has it.

Are you planning to sit the Model User exam this year?

Source: https://www.omg.org/certification/sysml2/

### Option 2 — Industry/trend angle (The displacement)

The OCSMP programme ran for nearly two decades. OMG just launched a separate certification series for SysML v2, and both series will operate in parallel through the transition period.

That parallel period is where the risk lives. Programmes will have practitioners who trained on v1 tooling sitting alongside people certifying on v2. The credentials look similar from the outside. The underlying language and modelling approach are not comparable.

OMG chose not to retire OCSMP yet, which is the right call given how few production tools fully support v2 today. But parallel credential tracks during a language transition tend to slow convergence rather than accelerate it. Organisations will defer the decision about which standard to train against, and that deferral compounds quietly until a programme feels it.

The certification launch is good news. The transition plan it implies is worth scrutinising before your programme commits to a training path.

Source: https://www.omg.org/certification/sysml2/

### Option 3 — Contrarian angle

The SysML v2 certification launched this week. The tooling to actually practise SysML v2 at programme scale still does not exist in any mature form.

Certifying practitioners before the tools are ready is not inherently wrong. The demand signal may accelerate tool development. But there is a real cost: engineers who certify now will learn the language largely through reference implementations and pilot environments, not through the messy reality of programme use.

OCSMP had this problem too. Plenty of certified practitioners could not construct a useful model under programme pressure because the exam tested language knowledge, not modelling judgement. A ground-up redesign of the cert does not automatically fix that.

I hope the SysML v2 exam is harder than OCSMP was. The community needs practitioners who can model, not practitioners who can pass a language quiz.

### Option 4 — Balanced breakdown

SysML v2 now has an official certification programme, and the first credential is already open for registration.

OMG launched the entry-level "Model User" credential on June 2, 2026. It targets practitioners who need to read and interpret SysML v2 models: systems engineers, architects, analysts, and project managers. The "Model Builder Fundamental" tier follows in early 2027, with Intermediate and Advanced credentials after that. The programme runs independently of the existing OCSMP series, which stays active during the transition.

For practitioners on a v2 adoption path, this is a concrete career marker that did not exist six months ago. People with direct involvement in the spec developed the exam content, which gives it more credibility than a third-party interpretation.

The honest complication: the full credential ladder does not exist yet, and the tool ecosystem is still maturing. Certifying at Model User level today is low-risk. Planning a team-wide training programme around a series that is only partly built carries more uncertainty.

File this one if you are mapping out MBSE workforce development for the next 18 months.

## POST 2 — DoD as Diamond Sponsor / Policy & Procurement Signal

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle

The U.S. Department of Defense is the Diamond Sponsor of the new SysML v2 certification programme. That is not a neutral endorsement.

Daniel Hettema, the DoD's Director of Digital Engineering, Modeling and Simulation, described SysML v2 certification as ensuring "practitioners across the industrial base remain aligned and fully equipped to collaborate." The phrase "industrial base" is doing real work in that sentence. This framing extends beyond government engineers and prime contractors to the whole supply chain.

If you work on defence programmes, especially in the U.S. market, pay attention. Diamond-level sponsorship with explicit industrial base language is how a soft expectation becomes a contract requirement over the next procurement cycle or two.

The credential series is new and the upper tiers do not exist yet. Waiting to see how this plays out before planning your team's training path is a reasonable way to fall behind.

### Option 3 — Contrarian angle

The DoD calling SysML v2 certification an "industrial base benchmark" before Model Builder even exists is a procurement signal dressed up as a workforce development initiative.

The Model User credential tests whether someone can read a model. That is a foundational skill, but reading comprehension is not the capability gap that breaks digital engineering programmes. The gap is practitioners who can construct, maintain, and govern models under programme pressure. That tier of the certification does not launch until early 2027 at the earliest.

Anchoring industrial base readiness to an incomplete credential series tells you something about the timeline pressure DoD is under. A credential series is a useful tool. Treating a partial one as a readiness benchmark because the spec is finally published is optimistic accounting.
