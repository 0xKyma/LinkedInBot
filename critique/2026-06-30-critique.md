## Obeo Strengthens Its Commitment to the Future of Capella (June 10, 2026)

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle
Capella 7.1 is coming with Java 21, security hardening, and i18n, plus an agent-ready Capella built on an MCP/API layer.

If you run Capella today, two things matter here. The Java 21 move means you should plan your runtime upgrade now, not after the release lands. And the API/MCP work makes your model addressable by external tools, including AI agents, so you no longer script around the GUI.

The first commercial AI release is planned for end of July. Obeo frames AI as something that assists the modeller and does not replace the modelling decision. I think that framing holds up.

Pilot the API integration before the AI features ship. The agent-ready interface is the part that survives whatever the AI hype cycle does next.

### Option 2 — Industry/trend angle (the money signal)
Obeo is putting commercial money behind AI in Capella, and the timing tells you where the open-source MBSE market is heading.

The June post commits direct contributions to Capella 7.1 and an agent-ready architecture, with a paid AI release at end of July. They also signalled tighter work with CEA-List on Papyrus Web and SysON.

### Option 3 — Contrarian angle
Everyone wants AI in their MBSE tool. Almost no one has the model quality to make it useful.

Obeo's agent-ready Capella and end-of-July AI release are well scoped. The framing is assistant, not replacement. I agree with that framing. But the assistant only works if the model underneath it is consistent, well-structured, and actually maintained.

Most Capella models I have reviewed are not in that state. They have orphaned elements, half-applied methodology, and diagrams that drifted from the underlying data years ago. Point an AI agent at that and you get confident answers built on a mess.

AI does not create that risk. Teams create it when they treat AI features as a reason to skip the modelling discipline they already skip.

If your model cannot survive a human reviewer asking hard questions, it will not survive an agent either.

## SysML 2.0b2 Open Issue — Tabular View Interchange Gap (May 21, 2026)

### Option 1 — Practitioner angle
A SysML v2 b2 ballot issue filed in May says the spec barely covers tabular views, and that matters more than it sounds.

The filing notes the spec has "only very sporadic mention of tabular-type views" and warns there will be "no chance of successful tabular view interchange between the tools" without proper view-modelling guidance.

Tables are how most of us actually share model content. Requirements tables, allocation matrices, interface lists. That is what program reviews consume, not the abstract syntax.

So if tabular views are underspecified, exporting a table from one SysML v2 tool and reading it correctly in another is not guaranteed. The data interchange works. The view does not travel.

If you are picking a SysML v2 toolchain now, test table round-tripping between tools before you commit. Do not assume it works because the model interchange does.

Source: issues.omg.org/issues/spec/SysML

#MBSE #SysML #SystemsEngineering

### Option 2 — Industry/trend angle (the adoption gap)
SysML v2's biggest selling point is tool interoperability. A live spec issue shows that promise has a gap right at the surface.

A b2 ballot issue from May reports that the spec barely covers tabular views, and warns this blocks reliable view interchange between tools.

Look at the distance between capability and practice. The model semantics are standardised and interchangeable. But the views people actually read, tables most of all, fall outside what the spec fully covers. So two conformant tools can hold the same model and present it differently, or fail to exchange the view at all.

That gap is what keeps real adoption cautious. Engineers work in tables and diagrams, and those have to travel too.

Until the spec fully covers view modelling, "interoperable" means interoperable data, not interoperable deliverables. Those are not the same thing to a program manager.

Source: issues.omg.org/issues/spec/SysML
